Behavioral Threat Assessment with Digital Forensics

Available SCORM e-learning

Threat assessment teams typically fail to gain access to the information most relevant to determining dangerousness. The information on the student's phone. This program covers how to bring that material into a BTA process lawfully — under consent or court order — and how to read it against established assessment models.

The boundaries are the curriculum, not a footnote to it: what may be examined, under what authority, and where the process stops.

ACT

Assess · Choose · Tell In development

A civilian response protocol built as a sequence: assess, then choose, then tell. Three steps in order rather than a set of actions to recall under pressure.

  1. Assess Before you can determine the best response, you have to assess the situation.
  2. Choose Lockdown, evacuation, or resistance — the option that fits where you actually are. ACT integrates with the protocols a district already runs, including Standard Response Protocol and Run-Hide-Fight.
  3. Tell Alert others nearby, contact 911, and assist responders as they arrive.

ACT provides age-appropriate solutions for pre-K through 12, and emphasizes situational awareness and repetition over high-stress scenarios.

SAMAP

samap.ai In development

Situational Awareness Map. Upload an aerial photo and a floor plan, drag them into alignment, and drop in labeled points — entrances, staging areas, command posts, hazards, rally points. Export a single PDF a responder can read on a hood.

A Smart Grid overlay gives every location a shared reference, so the map and the radio traffic agree.

The Origin of SAMAP

On October 19, 2000, I was on the Governor's Detail working with the US Secret Service Advance Team, ahead of a visit from President Bill Clinton and Vice President Al Gore. POTUS and VPOTUS were coming to attend the funeral of Missouri Governor Mel Carnahan, who had died days earlier along with his son Randy and aide Chris Sifford in a plane crash.

During that advance, I was introduced to the concept of applying a grid reference to an image — a way of breaking a physical space down into referenceable coordinates so that anyone communicating about that space could point to the exact same location without ambiguity.

Online Tabletop Exercise

In development

A browser-based platform for building, delivering and reporting tabletop exercises. The builder follows the Homeland Security Exercise and Evaluation Program (HSEEP) framework.

In delivery, the facilitator runs a presenter view and screen-shares it through whatever meeting tool they choose. The platform captures decisions and discussion as the exercise runs and builds the after action report.

Loaded with a robust scenario library, including behavioral threat assessment scenarios, rather than an empty builder.
